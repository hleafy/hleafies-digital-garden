### Core Premise: Humans are "Ensembles of Social Relations"

Marx famously stated that _"the human essence is no abstraction inherent in each single individual. In its reality it is the ensemble of the social relations."_ This is the master key.

What does this mean?

- **Not Atomistic Individuals:** Humans are not isolated, pre-formed atoms who then come together to form society (as in liberal philosophy). We are born into a pre-existing social world that shapes us fundamentally.
    
- **Formed by Activity:** We create ourselves and our world through our collective activity—primarily **labor** or **praxis**.
    

---

### Key Pillars of the Marxist Analysis of Humanity

#### 1. Humans as Practical, Laboring Beings (Homo Faber)

This is the starting point. What distinguishes humans from animals is not just consciousness, but **conscious, purposeful activity to transform nature.**

- **Labor/Praxis:** We don't just adapt to the world; we remake it to meet our needs. In doing so, we also _remake ourselves_—developing new skills, knowledge, and social forms.
    
- **Metabolism with Nature:** Labor is our ongoing, necessary interaction with nature. Our tools, technology, and economy are extensions of this process.
    

#### 2. Humans as Inherently Social Beings

Our labor is _social_ from the start. We cooperate, communicate, and organize to produce.

- **Production Requires Relations:** To farm, build, or create, we enter into specific **social relations of production** (master/slave, lord/serf, capitalist/wage-worker). These relationships are the core of any society's structure.
    
- **Consciousness is Social:** Even our thoughts, language, and desires are shaped by our social life. "It is not the consciousness of men that determines their existence, but their social existence that determines their consciousness."
    

#### 3. Humans as Historical Beings

There is no single, eternal "human nature." What it means to be human changes throughout history, depending on the **mode of production** (e.g., primitive communism, slavery, feudalism, capitalism).

- **Different Societies, Different Humans:** The senses, capacities, and self-understanding of a medieval artisan, a feudal peasant, and a modern factory worker are profoundly different because their social worlds and daily activities are different.
    
- **History is the Story of Human Self-Development:** History is the process through which humanity develops its powers and potential through changing its conditions of existence (albeit through conflict and struggle).
    

#### 4. Humans as Class Beings (Under Class Society)

In societies divided by class (where one group owns the means of production), your class position is the most decisive social relation shaping your life.

- **Your place in production** determines your material interests, your life experience, your worldview, and your potential for freedom or oppression.
    
- A capitalist's "humanity" is expressed and constrained by their role as an owner and accumulator of capital.
    
- A worker's "humanity" is expressed and constrained by their need to sell their labor-power to survive.
    

---

### The Central Problem: Alienation (Entfremdung)

This concept ties it all together and explains the _crisis_ of being human under capitalism.

Alienation is the state where the products and processes of our own creative labor come to dominate us, appearing as an external, hostile force. Under capitalism, it manifests in four key ruptures:

1. **Alienation from the Product of Labor:** The worker makes an object, but it is owned by the capitalist. The product becomes a commodity that stands _against_ the worker, a power they do not control.
    
2. **Alienation from the Act of Labor:** Work becomes external, not a fulfillment but a mere means to survive ("wage slavery"). It is not freely chosen or creative, but dictated and monotonous.
    
3. **Alienation from our "Species-Being" (Gattungswesen):** Our essential nature as creative, self-producing beings is negated. Labor, which should be our distinctive human activity, is reduced to animal-like activity for mere subsistence.
    
4. **Alienation from Other Humans:** Social relations become mediated by commodities and competition. Other people become means to an end, competitors, or representatives of an opposing class (boss vs. worker).
    

---

### The Goal: The Emancipated Human & Communism

Marxist analysis isn't just diagnostic; it's aimed at liberation. The goal is to overcome alienation by revolutionizing the social relations that cause it.

- **Communism** is envisioned not just as a fairer distribution of goods, but as the positive overcoming of alienation. It is the society where:
    
    - The **free development of each is the condition for the free development of all.**
        
    - The antagonism between individual and society is reconciled.
        
    - Labor becomes "life's prime want"—a form of self-realization, not just a means to live.
        
    - Humans gain **conscious collective control** over their social metabolism with nature, ending the anarchy of the market and class rule.
        

### In Summary: A Step-by-Step Framework

To analyze a human in the Marxist view, ask:

1. **What is their material context?** In what historical epoch and mode of production do they live?
    
2. **What is their class position?** Do they own/control the means of production, or must they sell their labor? This defines their fundamental social relation.
    
3. **What is the nature of their labor/activity?** Is it alienated? Is it a sphere of freedom or mere necessity? Does it allow for creativity and development?
    
4. **How are their consciousness and desires shaped?** By the ideologies that justify the existing social order? By the consumerism of capitalism? By the solidarity of class struggle?
    
5. **What is their potential for emancipation?** Given their position, are they a potential agent for transforming the dehumanizing social relations, moving humanity towards a more fulfilling stage of history?
    

This framework shifts the question from "What _is_ a human?" to "**What social conditions create truly human, free, and flourishing individuals, and what conditions cripple and alienate them?**" That is the powerful, practical heart of the Marxist analysis.
